# Introduction

Neurological conditions are the leading cause of ill health and disability
worldwide. The Global Burden of Disease 2021 analysis estimated that 3.4
billion people—43.1% of the global population—were living with a neurological
condition in 2021, while the associated disability-adjusted life years
increased by approximately 18% from 1990. Dementia is among the major
contributors to this burden, and neurodegenerative diseases have growing
consequences for individuals, families, health systems and economies.

The scale and heterogeneity of this evidence make transparent, continuously
updated research infrastructure essential. This need is consistent with the
WHO call to strengthen neurological research and data systems and with UN
Sustainable Development Goal 3, particularly Target 3.4 on non-communicable
diseases and mental health and Target 3.b on research and development.

Scientific basis: [GBD 2021 neurological burden — WHO
summary](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-03-2024-over-1-in-3-people-affected-by-neurological-conditions--the-leading-cause-of-illness-and-disability-worldwide),
[WHO Intersectoral Global Action Plan on neurological
disorders](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240076624), and [UN
Sustainable Development Goal 3](https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal3).

The Neurodegenerative Disease Research Observatory (NDRO) is a versioned,
auditable interface for exploring public PubMed evidence associated with five
neurodegenerative-disease groups represented in the current pilot snapshot:
Alzheimer's disease (AD), amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), frontotemporal
dementia (FTD), Huntington's disease (HD), and Parkinson's disease (PD).

NDRO is a living corpus. Records move through eligibility screening,
scientific-relevance classification and semantic extraction at different
speeds. Every public release is therefore a frozen snapshot with an explicit
version and cutoff date. A later snapshot can add, complete or reclassify a
record without modifying an earlier release.

## What NDRO is designed to support

- transparent browsing of publication–disease associations;
- explicit separation of candidate, analytical and semantic denominators;
- inspection of stored classification explanations and literal evidence spans;
- reproducible download of a named public snapshot;
- traceable community reports of possible classification errors.

## Mission

NDRO's mission is to make the public evidence landscape for
neurodegenerative diseases easier to inspect, reproduce and improve. It
connects aggregate indicators to their underlying publication–disease
associations, documents exclusions and uncertainty, and invites traceable
community corrections without changing canonical classifications
automatically.

## What NDRO does not claim

NDRO is not a clinical decision-support tool, a systematic-review conclusion,
or a causal treatment-effect estimator. Its evidence boundary is public PubMed
metadata and adequate English-language scientific abstracts. Full text is not
routinely assessed in the current release.
